# Medical Research Profile

Read this file for medical, biomedical, clinical, epidemiological, translational, or public-health
brainstorming. Use it with the current operation manual or leader campaign; the general isolation,
lifecycle, evidence, and promotion rules remain in force.

## Purpose

Make medical ideation scientifically discriminating by routing the question first and selecting
the dimensions that can change whether an idea should be retained, tested, narrowed, or stopped.

Medical mode keeps association, prediction, mechanism, intervention effect, and clinical
recommendation as distinct claims, each limited to the support available for that claim.

## Record the question type

Choose the closest useful type in `BRIEF.md`; combine types only when the objective genuinely
crosses them. Use the following list as routing guidance and extend it when another question type
better fits the objective.

| Question type | Decision-critical dimensions |
|---|---|
| Etiology or causal effect | population and time origin; exposure and outcome definitions; temporality; confounding, selection, and measurement; competing explanations; identifiable contrast |
| Diagnosis or screening | intended use and setting; target condition; index information; reference process; spectrum and verification effects; validation, calibration, and clinical consequences |
| Prognosis or prediction | target population and time zero; prediction horizon; outcome; candidate information availability; overfitting and optimism; calibration, validation, competing events, and usefulness |
| Intervention | eligible population; intervention and comparator; clinically meaningful outcomes and timing; adherence and contamination; safety, equipoise, feasibility, and implementation context |
| Mechanism or pathobiology | biological level and context; perturbation or natural contrast; alternative mechanisms; discriminating prediction; model-system limitations; human relevance and falsification route |
| Translation | clinical need; target or product plausibility; delivery or exposure; safety window; sample, tissue, manufacturing, or workflow feasibility; path from mechanism to patient-relevant test |
| Secondary data, omics, or methods | analytic unit; data and variable availability; sample size or event information; measurement and missingness; multiplicity, leakage, batch or center effects; reproducibility and claim boundary |
| Implementation or health systems | stakeholders and setting; workflow and adoption; context dependence; access and equity; implementation outcomes; sustainability and unintended effects |

Use PICO when it clarifies an intervention or structured clinical question; otherwise use the
structure appropriate to the question. Evaluate each source against the claim it is meant to
support rather than through one universal ranking of study designs.

## Build medical success criteria

Translate the selected question type into a small set of observable `SC-*` criteria. Include only
dimensions that could change the decision. Common candidates are:

- a clinically or scientifically meaningful decision or knowledge gap;
- a clearly bounded population, unit, setting, and time origin;
- an exposure, intervention, predictor, mechanism, or implementation object that can be defined;
- an outcome or discriminating observation that could support or weaken the idea;
- important bias, causal, measurement, or alternative-explanation boundaries;
- accessible data, samples, events, tools, or experiments;
- ethical, safety, privacy, or equity constraints when they are material;
- a claim strength that matches the available evidence and study design;
- a concrete falsifier or highest-information next test.

Select only the dimensions that can change the decision. A mechanism project and a diagnostic
study therefore receive different gates when their claims require them.

## Diversify medical directions

For a campaign, assign different primary contributions before dispatch. Depending on the brief,
use axes such as:

- a competing mechanism or causal explanation;
- a different population, phenotype, disease stage, or boundary condition;
- an alternative measurement, outcome, or time scale;
- a bias, artifact, null, or selection explanation;
- a different data source, study design, perturbation, or validation route;
- a translational, implementation, or feasibility route;
- a repair for a weakness identified by review.

Judge duplication by the primary mechanism or implementation structure, differentiating
prediction, and practical validation route rather than by a shared disease name or parent
proposition.

Assign workers substantively different axes rather than stylistic variations of one fashionable
mechanism. The brief determines which axes deserve coverage in a particular campaign.

## Evidence and feasibility discipline

During VERIFY:

- match authoritative evidence to the claim type;
- distinguish direct evidence, indirect support, biological plausibility, and analogy;
- seek counterevidence and simpler explanations;
- check whether the required variables, samples, outcomes, time origin, and analytic unit actually
  exist before claiming feasibility;
- preserve uncertainty when novelty, extractability, bias, or implementation remains unresolved;
- keep association, prediction, mechanism, intervention effect, and clinical recommendation as
  different claims;
- use the general evidence gate plus only the profile-specific dimensions activated by the brief.

An unavailable dataset variable, inaccessible specimen, unresolved reference standard, or unsafe
intervention may block or narrow an idea without automatically making its scientific proposition
false. Use `blocked`, `weakened`, a frozen expansion state, or a reopen condition as appropriate.

## Review metadata

An accepted review may list which `SC-*` criteria the selected idea:

- addresses;
- currently supports;
- currently blocks or leaves unresolved.

This metadata represents per-idea coverage. The leader combines current accepted review metadata
across the permitted portfolio view and makes the final campaign judgment.

## General-domain compatibility

For non-medical tasks, retain the same campaign and atomic-operation rules and derive
domain-appropriate success criteria and diversity axes from the shared brief. Software work may,
for example, distinguish architecture, algorithm, interface, operations, security, migration, or
validation directions without changing the brainstorm lifecycle.
